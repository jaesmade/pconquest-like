# Early-run balance baseline

The early run uses the standard-style damage calculation in `src/game/damage.ts` and derives HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, and Special Defense from base values and level. Speed is the species' base Speed at every level; Movement remains a fixed species tile range. The current 11-form catalog averages 63.27 Speed, close to the 65 timing reference. Damage rules, the 1-in-24 critical chance, type chart, weather, abilities, and one Attack command per turn remain unchanged. The scheduler uses `AV = 650,000 / effective Speed`; a 10,000-AV cycle gives a reference Speed-65 Pokémon one turn per cycle. Every Pokémon gains 3 AP per turn; Movement sets tile range, and one Move command costs 1 AP regardless of route length or terrain. This changes timing and action frequency substantially, so encounter balance remains subject to fresh play data.

## Proposed higher-floor level curve

Keep the table above as floor 1. For a future floor climb, add a separate floor index and a configurable enemy-level offset while preserving the current column, elite, and boss differences. A provisional starting curve is `floorOffset = 6 × (floorIndex - 1)`, added to the existing node level; it would put the floor 15 boss at level 105. Use a separate enemy ceiling (provisionally 999) while keeping every player Pokémon capped at level 100. The six-level offset is an estimate based on expected battle XP per floor and needs play data before it becomes final.

At enemy levels above 100, the existing HP and Attack/Defense formulas extrapolate from the species base stats; Speed and Movement remain fixed. Review high-level damage, HP totals, encounter duration, move availability, and the level gap between the party and each floor. Do not grant enemies new moves automatically: only authored learnset entries and their four equipped slots apply. The current level curve and floor-1 balance remain unchanged until the floor system is implemented and tested.

Only Pokémon deployed in a battle receive its XP, including deployed battlers that faint; reserves receive none. New runs begin at level 10 with a six-point party draft; current draft choices cost two points each, yielding three Pokémon. A run can own up to 20 Pokémon, while solo battles deploy up to six. Each current species starts with four equipped moves; later level unlocks can replace a slot on the XP screen. Recruits arrive at the highest level already in the party, so a late recruit is useful immediately. Bulbasaur and Squirtle can evolve at level 12 after enough battles. Early learned moves unlock at levels 11–13 rather than being granted immediately at level 10. A route with fewer battles earns less XP but can gain healing, items, or recruits.

At level `L`, HP is `floor(2 × base HP × L / 100) + L + 10`; Attack, Defense, Special Attack, and Special Defense are each `floor(2 × base stat × L / 100) + 5`; Speed and Movement stay at their species base values at every level. At level 10 the current base HP span of 52–78 becomes 30–35 HP. Basic damaging moves have 38–50 power; Thunderbolt is the 75-power, 4-AP area move. With equal attacking and defending stats, a 50-power STAB attack at level 10 deals 9–12 neutral damage or 18–24 damage on a 2× target before weather, abilities, or critical hits. Speed sets how quickly a Pokémon returns on the AV timeline; Movement caps tiles moved per command. The current 11-form catalog averages Speed 63.27 at every level; one 10,000-AV cycle matches one turn at reference Speed 65. Trick Room lasts five global cycles and changes timeline Speed to `4,225 / effective Speed`, reversing action order and frequency around Speed 65.

The first enemy trio is Meowth, Vulpix, and Squirtle. It offers multiple type answers without opening with Geodude's Electric immunity and high physical Defense. Later normal nodes rotate through authored maps and teams. Elite nodes add an opponent and three levels. The boss uses a stronger four-Pokémon team on Crown Citadel. No opponent is given a hidden damage bonus.

Unused AP banks in full within each battle, for either side. A unit adds 3 AP when it next acts. It can Move once (1 AP, within its Movement tile range) and Attack once per turn; move and Special actions retain their listed AP costs. This changes the value of passing early, so future balance reviews should record banked AP and whether delayed movement or item use changes encounter outcomes.

## Save behavior

Schema v25 switches Speed to the unchanged base value at every level; active battles from earlier schemas restart at preparation with a report because their saved battle units contain level-scaled Speed, while party, route, HP, and progression remain saved. Schema v24 calibrated Speed and AV timing; older active battles also restart under the v25 rule. Schema v23 added a saved Trick Room expiry timestamp; older active battles resumed with the field inactive. Schema v22 saves each unit's next action time, scheduled Speed, and per-turn Move use. v21 and earlier saves default the new movement-used flag to false; earlier active battles also convert their round-based turn queue and timed effects to the AV timeline while preserving the current actor. The migration retains earlier route and combat migrations; schema v14 preserved the v11 HP rescaling for older saves and migrates pre-v13 active Stealth Rock tiles to temporary legacy zones with their original expiry. Pre-v14 active stat stages are capped at ±6 and receive the five-cycle stage timer. A v11 battle resumes with each unit's Movement restored from its species record; battles saved before v11 return to preparation because their other stats used the older formula. Versions before v9 also retain their earlier level and XP migration. Current and recovery copies remain in IndexedDB.

## Next tuning evidence

This is a data pass, not a claim of final difficulty. When play sessions are requested, record win rate by starting draft, route path, number of battles, recruited party size, cycles and elapsed time per encounter, extra turns gained from Speed breakpoints, damage taken, fainted allies, store purchases, and how often a move or item is selected. Look especially at parties that reach the boss after few battles, Lapras under snow/rain, Pikachu's 2×2 Thunderbolt, and the sandstorm boss. Tune encounter data and move power from those observations before changing the damage formula.
