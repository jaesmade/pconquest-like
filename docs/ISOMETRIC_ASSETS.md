# Isometric battlefield asset guide

The battle presents the existing square rules grid in a three-quarter isometric view. Movement, range, line of sight, weather, and damage still use integer `(x, y)` cells. `src/battle/isometric.ts` owns world coordinates and click conversion. Phaser draws tiles, highlights, routes, units, and attack effects through that projection; the React HUD uses it to place the contextual popup and minimap.

The [battle preview](ISOMETRIC_BATTLE_PREVIEW.png) captures the first campaign encounter at 1440×900, with allies entering from the bottom grid edge and opponents from the top. Unit models and portraits in that capture are still the named placeholder sheets.

## Visual frame

- Each tile top is a **96×48 pixel diamond**. Adjacent grid cells move 48 pixels sideways and 24 pixels downward in screen space.
- Elevation lifts a tile top by **20 pixels per level**. The tile art extends its earth or stone sides so all levels meet the same lower plane. The current map contract permits levels 0–2.
- The board has a deep navy surrounding color. Grass uses bright yellow-green tops over warm brown cliffs, water uses blue tops and lighter foam, lava uses orange crust, and walls use gray stone. Decorative trees, rocks, and flowers use the same blocky pixel style.
- Gameplay overlays remain distinct: translucent green movement diamonds, blue move range, gold target/path, and colored effectiveness outlines. These are drawn by Phaser so art replacements cannot alter their meaning.
- Campaign maps put the ally deployment zone in the bottom two grid rows and the enemy zone in the top two, with four neutral rows between. Default allies start in the bottom row and enemies in the top row. Battle Lab uses the same top/bottom layout on a smaller grid. Allies face north and enemies face south at deployment; gameplay direction is based on grid axes.
- Drag the battlefield with the left mouse button to pan the camera. A short press and release still selects a tile. Right or middle dragging also pans; the mouse wheel and camera controls change zoom. The camera allows a small margin around the map so dragging is visible even when the whole map fits the viewport.

## Replaceable files

The runtime asset map is [isometric-manifest.json](../public/assets/environment/isometric/isometric-manifest.json). The initial family consists of `iso-<kind>-h<level>-96px.svg` for `plain`, `water`, `lava`, and `wall` at levels 0–2, plus named tree, rock, flower, bush, and grass-tuft SVGs. All are original pixel assets generated by `scripts/generate_isometric_assets.mjs`; the script skips existing files unless run with `--force`.

To replace a tile, preserve its **96-pixel width**, the diamond top corners `(48,0)`, `(96,24)`, `(48,48)`, `(0,24)`, and the height-specific side depth of `22 + 20 × level` pixels. Keep the outer background transparent and test neighboring tiles as a 3×3 patch. New art can be PNG: change its URL in the manifest and load it with `this.load.image` instead of `this.load.svg` in `src/battle/Board.tsx`. Keep texture IDs stable or update the renderer lookup too. Decorations are visual only and do not provide cover or block movement.

The title background is [title-island-reference-inspired.png](../public/assets/backgrounds/title-island-reference-inspired.png). It was generated with the built-in image tool using the user-provided image as a **style and perspective reference** and saved into this project. Final prompt: “Create a wide 16:9 decorative title-screen scene of a floating grassy island with chunky brown earth cliffs, a bright blue river, a short waterfall, rounded leafy trees, scattered rocks and tiny flowers, surrounded by deep navy negative space. Isometric 3/4 view, crisp deliberate pixel clusters, saturated greens and turquoise water, soft dark vignette, readable silhouettes. Keep the center quiet for an HTML menu. No characters, Pokémon, text, UI, logos, or watermark.”

Pokémon battle sheets and attack effect sheets keep their existing named paths and animation manifest. Their frames remain 32×32; the renderer places their feet on projected tile centers. The placeholder portraits and Pokémon sheets can be replaced independently using [the animation guide](ANIMATION_ASSETS.md). Character art is still placeholder art, so its detail level does not yet match the new terrain.

## Current rendering limit

Terrain and decorations are stamped once into a static render texture for map sizes up to 32×32. This keeps tile count out of the frame loop. The single terrain layer stays behind all unit sprites, so a tree canopy will not hide a Pokémon walking behind it. If foreground occlusion becomes important, split decorations into depth-sorted objects after measuring 32×32 performance.
