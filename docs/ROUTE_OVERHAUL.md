# Ten-column route and deployment overhaul

## Run flow

After the six-point starting draft, the player enters a generated cavern route with **10 progression columns** displayed as successive rows climbing from bottom to top. Column 1 is a battle near the bottom; column 10 is always the boss at the highest point. Columns 2–9 have between two and four choices spread across the board width and a mix of battle, elite, healing, store, and special nodes. Columns 4 and 7 always contain four nodes so every generated run visibly uses the expanded branching layout. A choice is legal only when it is in the next progression column and has a drawn connection from the previously completed node. The full map can be inspected by vertical scrolling or dragging upward to the boss, while only connected choices in the next column are active. Route generation uses a separate seeded stream, so opening the screen does not change combat RNG.

Columns 2, 3, 5, and 7 guarantee visible healing, store, special, or recruitment opportunities. Column 5 guarantees a recruitment node; other node positions vary with the run seed.

| Node | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Battle | Three standard enemies. Win for shared XP and 12 coins. |
| Elite battle | Four enemies at a higher level. Win for shared XP and 20 coins. |
| Healing | Immediately fills HP and revives every owned Pokémon, including reserves. |
| Store | Opens a shop. Spend coins on held items or single-use TMs for the bag; leave when ready. |
| Special | A weighted encounter rolls when entered: 75% chance of a positive result and 25% chance of a negative result. Positive results include 50–100 coins in 10-coin steps, a Sitrus Berry, or healing 25% HP across the roster. Negative results remove half the coins or deal 20% HP damage across the roster. The rolled result is saved until claimed. |
| Recruitment | Choose one of three free Pokémon. The choices include both starter-pool and non-starter species, including rare Vulpix and Charmander. At the 20-owned cap, choose one to release first; held items return to the bag and selected party slots transfer. |
| Boss | Four high-level opponents on the Crown Citadel map. Defeat them and hold the capture tile to clear the layer and finish this run. Win for shared XP and 30 coins. |

The shop starts with Sitrus Berry (12), X Attack (14), Leftovers (20), Assault Vest (26), TM Swift (16), TM Thunderbolt (28), and Charizardite X (40). A new run starts with 20 coins and one TM Swift in the bag. Bought items enter the bag. Held items are assigned from Party; TMs are used from Bag while choosing the next route node. Shops may sell multiple copies while funds remain. Prices and stock live in `src/content/shop.ts`.

Normal enemy level is `9 + floor((column − 1) × 0.8)`; elite adds 3 levels and one opponent. The boss currently uses level 21 and four opponents. Battle wins give 65 XP at normal nodes, 85 at elite nodes, and 100 at the boss. These values are initial tuning targets, not measured balance.

## Preparation on the map

While choosing a route node, open **Party** to review every owned Pokémon's four active moves and to change compatible held items or evolve an eligible Pokémon. The active moves are read-only there. Item swaps return the old item to the bag. Evolution preserves all existing active moves and their slots; it adds the evolved form's available moves to learned history. The adjacent **Bag** control separates held items from Technical Machines, with grouped quantities and descriptions. A TM is used from Bag by choosing a compatible recipient and the active slot to replace; it is consumed only after that choice. TM Swift is a TM-only acquisition, while Thunderbolt can also be gained by Pikachu on level-up. Evolution can be deferred to a later route choice.

After choosing a battle, elite, or boss node, preparation shows a scrollable owned roster beside the **authored isometric map**. Up to six healthy Pokémon can be selected. Select a roster card, then click or keyboard-activate a highlighted tile in the ally zone. Each roster row shows a portrait, level, type, HP, deployment number and state, and an Add or Remove action. The six-segment meter shows how many may enter battle; the active Pokémon has a distinct outline and status. The map uses the same tile assets, tile centers, zones, terrain objects, and legal-placement rule as combat. Its enemy and ally labels reinforce the top/bottom orientation, and the active unit's current tile has a brighter border. Selected positions show each species' north-facing idle sprite frame, ground shadow, and a small deployment number. Flying units are lifted above their shadow; swimmers show a ripple. These are the same placeholder action sheets used in battle, so portraits remain in the roster cards only. Clicking an occupied tile swaps two teammates only when both positions are legal. Enemy entry remains in the top enemy zone. Preparation handles deployment and positioning only.

Preparation now opens over a noninteractive view of the route board. A dark translucent frame holds the roster on the left and the live isometric deployment map on the right, matching the route selector's floor, tile graph, and panel colors. The roster uses full-width rows on desktop, while the map has a short mission heading and a clear positioning status beside the Enter battle action. Roster cards and the route Party panel have HP bars so the health of candidate units can be compared quickly. The map view is framed closer around the authored terrain, with slightly larger unit sprites and clearer legal, occupied, and active-tile borders. The frame stays within the viewport; at shorter desktop heights it compacts the heading and map so the Enter battle action remains visible. The upper-right Back button returns to route choice and removes that pending node from the visited path; its battle has not started. On narrow screens, the roster becomes a horizontally scrollable strip above the map without widening the frame beyond the viewport. The placement and six-unit limits still use the existing deployment rules.

After a victory, the growth screen uses the same route-board backdrop and frame. Its summary shows the encounter's XP award for every owned Pokémon, coins earned, and rewarded roster count. A scrollable two-column card grid (one column on narrow screens) shows portrait, level, awarded XP, level progress, HP, and deployed/reserve state. Over about 1.35 seconds, each card counts its XP award and fills the bar from the prior XP value to the saved final XP value; the level and bar wrap at a crossed level threshold, followed by a level-up badge and reward pop. This display animation does not change saved XP or combat results. Newly eligible level moves are offered one at a time below the cards. The player replaces one active move or keeps the current set, and every offer must be resolved before OK can advance. Pending offers are saved, so refresh does not bypass the choice. The award comes from the encounter definition used by battle completion; reserves receive it too. At the level cap, the progress display says Maximum level. Reduced-motion preferences show the final values immediately.

Store, special encounter, recruitment, and run-result screens use the route's repeating tile floor, dark translucent frame, and pale inner panel. Store and stop panels show the column and current coins; the store uses a two-column item grid on wide screens and one column on narrow screens. Recruitment choices show Pokémon portraits; special encounters show the rolled result and a claim action. Their controls use the same raspberry outline and gold primary action as the other menus.

The six-point **starting draft** still usually yields three Pokémon because ordinary species cost two points. Six is the battle deployment ceiling, not a larger starting budget. The run may own up to 20, with reserves outside battle. For implementation instructions on adding and changing route nodes or event outcomes, see the [route node and event authoring guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md).

## State, saves, and art

- `src/game/route.ts` defines node IDs, seeded generation, connection rules, and plan validation. Column 5 guarantees a recruitment node; specials remain guaranteed in columns 3 and 7. The [route node and event authoring guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md) documents how to extend these rules and migrate saved graphs.
- `src/game/engine.ts` validates node choices and resolves node rewards, battle strength, shop purchases, and progression. The saved run carries route nodes, visited node IDs, current node ID, and coins.
- `src/ui/RouteScreen.tsx` draws the graph and node icons as scalable SVG with keyboard focus. The full-screen selector uses larger raised tiles with pale stone sides, engraved pixel-grain tops, blue connections, cyan glow and amber pointers for available nodes, a clean repeating isometric tile floor, and a downward-pointing 3D pyramid marker above the current position. The marker uses a fixed diamond base with two shaded tapered faces; it bobs up and down without rotating. Reduced-motion settings disable the bob. Compact coin/Party/Bag controls sit at the upper left, with a small upper-right back button inspired by the supplied reference. A compact guide at the lower left shows current progression and details for the hovered or focused node; its up button pans toward the boss and its down button pans toward the start. Tile colors distinguish encounters and stops. Mouse dragging and touch or wheel scrolling reveal every higher row through the boss, while a short click still chooses an available node. When the viewport changes, it aligns the current tile and next choice in view. The start tile, first battle, and boss share the center line; successive route columns are spaced 340 SVG units upward, and choice lanes are spaced 230 units across. The responsive translucent party and inventory panels open from their respective controls. `src/ui/RouteStopScreen.tsx` renders shop and special choices. `src/ui/DeploymentBoard.tsx` draws the isometric placement map from the authored tiles.
- The route HUD uses one small coin row above equally sized Party and Bag buttons, keeping the map clear at desktop and narrow widths. Party uses a code-native pixel emblem and displays the owned roster count against the shared 20-Pokémon limit; Bag uses a pixel satchel and shows the number of unequipped items. The Travelling Team panel presents a compact, type-accented card for each owned Pokémon with portrait, level or fainted state, type, HP, ability, held item, and four read-only equipped moves. Manage Pokémon expands the held-item selector and any available evolution. Three cards fit across the usual desktop viewport, two across medium widths, and one across narrow widths; the panel scrolls internally for larger rosters. Inventory groups held items and TMs separately, with quantity, description, recipient and replacement controls for TMs, and a list of equipped items by Pokémon. Both panels scroll internally, support Escape and backdrop dismissal, and return keyboard focus to the opening control. Route-side team management remains separate from battle placement.
- Save schema **v20** adds persisted weighted special encounters and migrates existing route graphs to include recruitment nodes. Save v19 and earlier runs preserve their visited progress; an already-open legacy special stop becomes a 50-coin claim. Save schema v19 added persisted level-up move choices; older saves migrated without retroactive offers.
- `public/assets/backgrounds/route-tiles.svg` is the active route-selection backdrop: a small repeating pale limestone diamond grid with subtle tile variation and no ruins, vegetation, or other objects. `src/styles/route.css` repeats it at 112×56 CSS pixels, so it fills wide and narrow route viewports without cropping or stretching. The older `route-ruins-pixel.png` artwork remains in the assets folder but is not used by the selector. `public/assets/backgrounds/route-cavern-pixel.png` remains in the asset folder but is not the active route-stop backdrop. Graph lines, icons, and labels are code-native and replaceable without changing route rules.
- `public/assets/ui/route-trainer-placeholder.svg` remains in the asset folder but is no longer used by the route selector. The current-position marker is drawn as code-native SVG facets in `src/ui/RouteScreen.tsx`, so its shape and bobbing animation can be changed without replacing party Pokémon sprites.

## Review targets

- Follow a legal path from column 1 to column 10; locked and disconnected nodes never activate.
- Verify healing restores fainted reserves, shop purchases spend exact coins and enter the bag, special outcomes follow the 75/25 positive-negative roll without recruiting Pokémon, and separate recruitment nodes offer three choices including non-starter species. At 20 owned, recruitment requires a valid replacement and returns that Pokémon's held item to the bag.
- Deploy six on separate legal ally tiles when the run has six healthy Pokémon; a seventh cannot be selected.
- Confirm battle preparation and the battle renderer show the same map locations for every chosen position.
- On the placement screen, select another Pokémon, move it to an empty legal tile, and swap with an occupied legal tile. Confirm the active outline and status follow the selected Pokémon, the old placement message clears when changing focus, and the selected count and Add/Remove actions remain legible at narrow width.
- Confirm generated columns contain no more than four nodes, columns 4 and 7 contain four, and every displayed node has at least one incoming and outgoing connection where applicable.
- Check the route, Party, and Bag overlays at desktop and narrow widths. On narrow screens the graph scrolls vertically, starts with the current tile below its next choice, and the party and inventory cards stack into one column. Drag or scroll upward from the bottom start through all ten progression rows to the boss at the top; dragging across an available node must not select it, while a short click still does. The reference-sized view should show the uninterrupted stone tile floor without ruins or props, the dark start tile, connected colored encounter tiles, blue links, compact upper-left coin/Party/Bag HUD, and upper-right back control without a large title banner over the route. Confirm that bag quantity and equipped-item lists update after an item assignment.
- On a route choice, confirm the Party panel's moves cannot be edited; equip and unequip held items, evolve an eligible Pokémon, and use a TM from Bag on a compatible recipient and chosen slot. After a level-up, confirm the XP screen requires a replacement or skip choice before route progression. Items purchased or returned from replacement become available at the next route choice.
- Review the shop, special, and result panels on the repeated tile floor at desktop and narrow widths; content should scroll within the translucent frame without losing its main action.
- Review XP cards for deployed and reserve Pokémon, a level-up, a learned move, and a maximum-level Pokémon. Check that the coin summary, milestone list, and OK action remain reachable with a full 20-Pokémon roster and reduced motion enabled.

### Route node art details

The selector uses code-native SVG route plaques in `RouteScreen.tsx`, shaped as shallow isometric diamonds to fit the map's repeating floor grid. A compact dark stone base, thin muted bezel, small side facets, subdued color inset, and light grain keep each node readable without the heavy box walls. Icons are centered and sized to leave a clear border around them. Battle, store, and special nodes use warm gold; elite nodes use rose red with a four-point spark; healing is green; recruitment uses blue; the start is navy. The sword, healing cross, cart, question mark, recruit group, and boss mark are replaceable inside `Glyph`. A future icon asset should fit roughly 65×55 SVG units around its center and keep high contrast against its tile. Route links are muted behind nodes; available nodes receive a brighter rim and amber pointer. Locked future nodes remain fully colored so the route is readable, while their click and keyboard activation stay disabled. The number and placement of choices still come from the saved route graph, so a generated four-choice column may be denser than the visual reference.
