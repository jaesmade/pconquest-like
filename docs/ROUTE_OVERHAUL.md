# Ten-column route and deployment overhaul

## Run flow

After the six-point starting draft, the player enters a generated cavern route with **10 progression columns** displayed as successive rows climbing from bottom to top. Column 1 is a battle near the bottom; column 10 is always the boss at the highest point. Columns 2–9 have between two and four choices spread across the board width and a mix of battle, elite, healing, store, and special nodes. Columns 4 and 7 always contain four nodes so every generated run visibly uses the expanded branching layout. A choice is legal only when it is in the next progression column and has a drawn connection from the previously completed node. The full map can be inspected by vertical scrolling or dragging upward to the boss, while only connected choices in the next column are active. Route generation uses a separate seeded stream, so opening the screen does not change combat RNG.

Columns 2, 3, 5, and 7 guarantee visible healing, store, special, or recruitment opportunities. Column 5 guarantees a recruitment node; other node positions vary with the run seed.

| Node | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Battle | 3–8 Normal enemies by level (3–5 on this floor). Win for XP for each deployed participant and 10 coins. |
| Elite battle | 1–3 Elites with 2× combat stats plus 3–5 Normals, at a higher level. Win for XP for each deployed participant and 20 coins. |
| Healing | Immediately fills HP and revives every owned Pokémon, including reserves. |
| Store | Opens a shop. Spend coins on held items or single-use TMs for the bag; leave when ready. |
| Special | A weighted encounter rolls when entered: 75% chance of a positive result and 25% chance of a negative result. Positive results include 50–100 coins in 10-coin steps, a Sitrus Berry, or healing 25% HP across the roster. Negative results remove half the coins or deal 20% HP damage across the roster. The rolled result is saved until claimed. |
| Recruitment | Choose one of three free Pokémon. The choices include both starter-pool and non-starter species, including rare Vulpix and Charmander. At the 20-owned cap, choose one to release first; held items return to the bag and selected party slots transfer. |
| Boss | One 3× Boss, 0–2 Elites, and 2–5 Normals; the current level-21 encounter fields eight on Ancient Heartwood. Defeat them and hold the capture tile to finish this run. Win for XP for each deployed participant and 30 coins. |

### Ranked battle compositions

The [ranked-enemy and initial moveset rules](PLAN.md#proposed-enemy-ranks-and-automatic-latest-four-movesets) are implemented. Enemy rank is per unit, so escorts retain their own rank's stats. Preparation, battle creation, and reloads use the same seed/node/level roster without consuming combat RNG. All seven arenas support eight legal enemy placements through authored starts and zone fallback. Preparation lists species, rank counts, level, and moves. New recruits/wild opponents initialize with their latest four current-level moves; owned Pokémon retain chosen slots, manual level-up choices, and TM teaching. Save v27 persists rank and preserves owned choices while active older battles restart at preparation.

### Proposed special-node quest chain

The current special node still uses the weighted encounter described above. A future quest design could make one designated `?` node (recommended: the guaranteed special in column 3) always show the initial prompt and grant a Gracidea flower. After that, each later `?` node the player enters gets one chance to reveal the Shaymin follow-up. A miss raises the chance at a later visited `?`; skipping a node does not count as a miss. If a follow-up misses and another quest is available, that same node can introduce the new quest instead of showing an ordinary encounter. The chance rises but never reaches 100%, so the player may reach the boss without finding Shaymin. One suggested starting curve doubles the odds after each miss, beginning at 20% (about 20%, 33%, 50%, 67%, 80% on successive checks); tune these values before implementation.

The quest catalog should support three or more concurrent chains and any number of steps per chain. For example, a three-node quest starts on one special node, advances one step on each of two later successful special-node events, and completes only after the third node; a miss does not advance it, and its chance counter resets when the next step begins. Each special node resolves at most one quest event: check one active quest in round-robin order, then on a miss start one eligible unstarted quest if possible. A successful follow-up takes priority, and the newly started quest can be pursued at later `?` nodes. If no quest starts or follow-ups apply, the usual weighted encounter remains available. Each quest and each current step keep separate progress and miss counts.

The flower would be saved as a key item, separate from battle items and TMs; Shaymin would be a story reward rather than a normal recruitment offer. At a full 20-Pokémon roster, the player would choose whom to release before Shaymin joins. Show the active hunt on the route screen so the player knows to keep choosing `?` nodes.

Another proposed branch quest starts when the player meets Rayquaza at a designated `?` node. At a later special node, Groudon and Kyogre are fighting; the player chooses to side with Groudon (battle Kyogre), side with Kyogre (battle Groudon), or fight both (they team up). After winning, the player gets the branch's key item and matching recruit: Red Orb → Groudon, Blue Orb → Kyogre, Meteorite → Rayquaza. Run each fight through the existing preparation and battle flow, save the chosen branch, and award only that branch's reward. See the [route event guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md) for battle loss, save, and full-roster handling.

This quest system is a proposal only; it does not change current node odds or rewards. The quest starter should be reachable in every run if it is intended to always be offered; follow-ups remain probabilistic by design. The [route node and event authoring guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md) describes the data-driven multi-quest and multi-step state, escalating odds, event priority, pending scenes, key-item separation, and recruitment handling.

### Planned artifact rewards

Artifacts are not part of the current route implementation. The planned artifact system uses a separate run-wide collection, independent of the Bag and Pokémon-held items. Legendary artifacts may only be acquired from the route's question-mark event nodes (`special` nodes); other artifact rarities and their sources remain to be decided. Offers and claimed rewards will need to be saved so a refresh cannot reroll or duplicate an artifact. See the [run-wide artifacts plan](ARTIFACTS_PLAN.md) for the catalog cap, rarity tiers, sample effects, and open decisions.

The route selector now includes an **Artifact Bag** control as a UI placeholder. Opening it displays “coming soon”; it does not show artifact inventory, grant artifacts, or apply artifact effects.

The shop starts with Sitrus Berry (12), X Attack (14), Leftovers (20), Assault Vest (26), TM Swift (16), TM Thunderbolt (28), Charizardite X (40), and Fire Stone (20). A new run starts with 0 coins and one TM Swift plus one Fire Stone in the bag, making the evolution-item flow available for testing. Bought items enter the bag. Held items are assigned from Party; TMs and evolution items are used from Bag while choosing the next route node. Shops may sell multiple copies while funds remain. Prices and stock live in `src/content/shop.ts`.

Normal enemy level is `9 + floor((column - 1) × 0.8)`; Elite adds 3 levels. Counts use the level-9-to-21 curve described in the linked rules. The Boss uses level 21 and one Boss + two Elites + five Normals. Battle wins give 65 XP per deployed participant at normal nodes, 85 at Elite nodes, and 100 at the Boss. Reserves receive no battle XP. These values are initial tuning targets, not measured balance.

Normal arenas rotate by column through Mossveil Grove, Fernroot Woods, Sunshade Thicket, Willowbrook Crossing, and Pinewatch Rise, repeating after the fifth forest. Elite nodes use Moonpool Clearing and the boss uses Ancient Heartwood. Their ramps, terraces, trails, and scenery are described in the [battlefield asset guide](ISOMETRIC_ASSETS.md); the two new maps are in this campaign rotation. The route graph, enemy templates, levels, and rewards use the existing rules.

### Proposed higher-floor enemy levels

The current ten-column route is one floor and its boss ends the run. A future floor climb should store a separate one-based `floorIndex`; do not derive it from the route column or `unlocks`. The player roster remains capped at level 100, while enemy encounters can scale past 100 on higher floors. Preserve the current encounter levels on floor 1, then add a tunable floor offset to the normal, elite, and boss node levels.

As a starting curve, add 6 enemy levels per cleared floor: `floorOffset = 6 × (floorIndex - 1)`. Apply that offset after the current node formula, so floor 1 stays unchanged and the floor 15 boss would be level 105 (`21 + 84`). This is a pacing estimate based on roughly six battle rewards per floor, not a tested balance result; tune it against party XP gain and battle outcomes. Keep enemy level generation bounded by a separate technical ceiling (provisionally 999), not the player cap. Floor continuation should preserve the run's party and route rewards; define the inter-floor HP recovery rule before implementing floor transitions.

Player Pokémon, including recruits, remain at or below level 100 and stop earning level XP there. Enemy stats use the existing level formula above 100, while Speed and Movement keep their current fixed species values. Enemy moves still come only from authored learnsets and the four-move loadout; level 100+ does not invent moves. This is a proposal only; current floor generation and the current end-of-run boss behavior are unchanged.

## Preparation on the map

While choosing a route node, open **Party** to review every owned Pokémon's four active moves and to change compatible held items or evolve an eligible Pokémon. The active moves are read-only there. Item swaps return the old item to the bag. Evolution preserves all existing active moves and their slots; it adds the evolved form's available moves to learned history. The adjacent **Bag** control separates held items, evolution items, and Technical Machines, with grouped quantities and descriptions. Press **Use item** to choose a compatible Pokémon; Fire Stone evolves Vulpix into Ninetales at any level and consumes one stone on selection. It plays the shared evolution scene, adjusts current HP by the form's maximum-HP difference for living Pokémon, and keeps the existing active moves. The old and new forms use the front-facing idle spritesheet animation in the scene; reduced-motion settings hold the idle sprite on its first frame. Canceling the picker leaves the item in the bag. Press **Use TM** in the Bag to open an eligible-Pokémon picker; choosing a recipient opens a second window listing the active moves that can be replaced. If the recipient has an open slot, that window offers to add the TM move without replacing one. The TM is consumed only after the player chooses a replacement or open slot. TM Swift is a TM-only acquisition, while Thunderbolt can also be gained by Pikachu on level-up. Evolution can be deferred to a later route choice.

After choosing a battle, elite, or boss node, preparation shows a scrollable owned roster beside the **authored top-down map**. Up to six healthy Pokémon can be selected. Select a roster card, then click or keyboard-activate a highlighted tile in the ally zone. Each roster row shows a portrait, level, type, HP, deployment number and state, and an Add or Remove action. The six-segment meter shows how many may enter battle; the active Pokémon has a distinct outline and status. The map uses the same tile assets, tile centers, zones, terrain objects, and legal-placement rule as combat. Its enemy and ally labels reinforce the top/bottom orientation, and the active unit's current tile has a brighter border. Selected positions show each species' north-facing idle sprite frame, ground shadow, and a small deployment number. Flying units are lifted above their shadow; swimmers show a ripple. These are the same placeholder action sheets used in battle, so portraits remain in the roster cards only. Clicking an occupied tile swaps two teammates only when both positions are legal. Enemy entry remains in the top enemy zone. Preparation handles deployment and positioning only.

Preparation now opens over a noninteractive view of the route board. A dark translucent frame holds the roster on the left and the live top-down deployment map on the right, matching the route selector's floor, tile graph, and panel colors. The roster uses full-width rows on desktop, while the map has a short mission heading and a clear positioning status beside the Enter battle action. Roster cards and the route Party panel have HP bars so the health of candidate units can be compared quickly. The map view is framed closer around the authored terrain, with slightly larger unit sprites and clearer legal, occupied, and active-tile borders. The frame stays within the viewport; at shorter desktop heights it compacts the heading and map so the Enter battle action remains visible. The upper-right Back button returns to route choice and removes that pending node from the visited path; its battle has not started. On narrow screens, the roster becomes a horizontally scrollable strip above the map without widening the frame beyond the viewport. The placement and six-unit limits still use the existing deployment rules.

After a victory, the growth screen uses the same route-board backdrop and frame. Its summary shows the encounter's XP award per deployed participant, coins earned, and number of deployed Pokémon rewarded. A scrollable two-column card grid (one column on narrow screens) shows portrait, level, that Pokémon's XP award (zero for reserves), level progress, HP, and deployed/reserve state. Over about 1.35 seconds, participant cards count their XP award and fill the bar from the prior XP value to the saved final XP value; the level and bar wrap at a crossed level threshold, followed by a level-up badge and reward pop. Reserve cards keep their XP bar and level unchanged. This display animation does not change saved XP or combat results. Newly eligible level moves are offered one at a time below the cards. The player replaces one active move or keeps the current set, and every offer must be resolved before OK can advance. Pending offers are saved, so refresh does not bypass the choice. The award comes from the encounter definition used by battle completion; reserves do not receive it. At the level cap, the progress display says Maximum level. Reduced-motion preferences show the final values immediately.

Store, special encounter, recruitment, and run-result screens use the route's repeating tile floor, dark translucent frame, and pale inner panel. Store and stop panels show the column and current coins; the store uses a two-column item grid on wide screens and one column on narrow screens. Recruitment choices show Pokémon portraits; special encounters show the rolled result and a claim action. Their controls use the same raspberry outline and gold primary action as the other menus.

The six-point **starting draft** still usually yields three Pokémon because ordinary species cost two points. Six is the battle deployment ceiling, not a larger starting budget. The run may own up to 20, with reserves outside battle. For implementation instructions on adding and changing route nodes or event outcomes, see the [route node and event authoring guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md).

## State, saves, and art

Route availability and visited-node membership are cached by route reference. Inventory quantities are indexed once per bag reference and reused by grouped Bag cards and held-item selectors, retaining catalog order. The growth screen caches participant membership, initial XP/level, maximum HP, and report data across its 40 ms animation ticks; only displayed XP and level progress depend on the tick. These implementation optimizations preserve item flows, rewards, and the rendered markup. See the [optimization audit](SCALABILITY_AUDIT.md) for verification and measurement limits.

- `src/game/route.ts` defines node IDs, seeded generation, connection rules, and plan validation. Column 5 guarantees a recruitment node; specials remain guaranteed in columns 3 and 7. The [route node and event authoring guide](ROUTE_NODE_EVENTS.md) documents how to extend these rules and migrate saved graphs.
- `src/game/engine.ts` validates node choices and resolves node rewards, battle strength, shop purchases, and progression. The saved run carries route nodes, visited node IDs, current node ID, and coins.
- `src/ui/RouteScreen.tsx` draws the graph and node icons as scalable SVG with keyboard focus. The full-screen selector uses raised, square-cut isometric cube tokens with pale stone sides, inset category-colored tops, dark lower shadows, muted connecting paths, and amber pointers for available nodes over a clean repeating isometric tile floor. A downward-pointing 3D pyramid marks the current position and bobs gently without rotating; reduced-motion settings disable the bob. Compact coin/Party/Bag controls sit at the upper left, with a small upper-right back button inspired by the supplied reference. A compact guide at the lower left shows current progression and details for the hovered or focused node; its up button pans toward the boss and its down button pans toward the start. Tile colors distinguish encounters and stops. Mouse dragging and touch or wheel scrolling reveal every higher row through the boss, while a short click still chooses an available node. When the viewport changes, it aligns the current tile and next choice in view. The start tile, first battle, and boss share the center line; successive route columns are spaced 340 SVG units upward, and choice lanes are spaced 230 units across. The responsive translucent party and inventory panels open from their respective controls. `src/ui/RouteStopScreen.tsx` renders shop and special choices. `src/ui/DeploymentBoard.tsx` draws the top-down placement map from the authored tiles.
- The route HUD uses one small coin row above equally sized Party and Bag buttons, with a full-width Artifact Bag placeholder below them. Party uses a code-native pixel emblem and displays the owned roster count against the shared 20-Pokémon limit; Bag uses a pixel satchel and shows the number of unequipped items. Artifact Bag opens a “coming soon” window and has no inventory or gameplay behavior yet. The Travelling Team panel presents a compact, type-accented card for each owned Pokémon with a code-native species portrait, level or fainted state, type, HP, ability, held item, and four read-only equipped moves. These portraits give each supported species its own recognizable shape instead of reusing the Bulbasaur placeholder across the roster; other portrait surfaces and battle units still use the shared placeholder set. Manage Pokémon expands the held-item selector and any available evolution. Choosing an evolution immediately applies the existing species, stats, HP, and move changes, then opens a modal scene with a charging glow, an old-to-new sprite transition, and a completion message. The scene can be skipped, returns focus to the evolved Pokémon's roster card when closed, and completes immediately with reduced motion enabled. The evolution scene still uses the shared idle spritesheet until species-specific unit art is authored. Three cards fit across the usual desktop viewport, two across medium widths, and one across narrow widths; the panel scrolls internally for larger rosters. Inventory groups held items and TMs separately, with quantity, description, and a list of equipped items by Pokémon. Using a TM opens a window of compatible party members; choosing one opens a second window with the active move slots to replace, or an open-slot option when fewer than four moves are equipped. The TM is consumed only when a move slot is chosen. The panels scroll internally, support Escape and backdrop dismissal, and return keyboard focus to the opening control. Route-side team management remains separate from battle placement.
- Save schema **v20** adds persisted weighted special encounters and migrates existing route graphs to include recruitment nodes. Save v19 and earlier runs preserve their visited progress; an already-open legacy special stop becomes a 50-coin claim. Save schema v19 added persisted level-up move choices; older saves migrated without retroactive offers.
- `public/assets/backgrounds/route-tiles.svg` is the active route-selection backdrop: a small repeating pale limestone diamond grid with subtle tile variation and no ruins, vegetation, or other objects. `src/styles/route.css` repeats it at 112×56 CSS pixels, so it fills wide and narrow route viewports without cropping or stretching. The older `route-ruins-pixel.png` artwork remains in the assets folder but is not used by the selector. `public/assets/backgrounds/route-cavern-pixel.png` remains in the asset folder but is not the active route-stop backdrop. Graph lines, icons, and labels are code-native and replaceable without changing route rules.
- `public/assets/ui/route-trainer-placeholder.svg` remains in the asset folder but is no longer used by the route selector. The current-position marker is drawn as code-native SVG facets in `src/ui/RouteScreen.tsx`, so its shape and bobbing animation can be changed without replacing party Pokémon sprites.

## Review targets

- Follow a legal path from column 1 to column 10; locked and disconnected nodes never activate.
- Verify healing restores fainted reserves, shop purchases spend exact coins and enter the bag, special outcomes follow the 75/25 positive-negative roll without recruiting Pokémon, and separate recruitment nodes offer three choices including non-starter species. At 20 owned, recruitment requires a valid replacement and returns that Pokémon's held item to the bag.
- Deploy six on separate legal ally tiles when the run has six healthy Pokémon; a seventh cannot be selected.
- Confirm battle preparation and the battle renderer show the same map locations for every chosen position.
- On the placement screen, select another Pokémon, move it to an empty legal tile, and swap with an occupied legal tile. Confirm the active outline and status follow the selected Pokémon, the old placement message clears when changing focus, and the selected count and Add/Remove actions remain legible at narrow width.
- Confirm generated columns contain no more than four nodes, columns 4 and 7 contain four, and every displayed node has at least one incoming and outgoing connection where applicable.
- Check the route, Party, and Bag overlays at desktop and narrow widths. On narrow screens the graph scrolls vertically, starts with the current tile below its next choice, and the party and inventory cards stack into one column. Drag or scroll upward from the bottom start through all ten progression rows to the boss at the top; dragging across an available node must not select it, while a short click still does. The reference-sized view should show the uninterrupted stone tile floor without ruins or props, the dark start cube, connected category-colored cube tokens, muted links, compact upper-left coin/Party/Bag HUD, and upper-right back control without a large title banner over the route. Confirm that bag quantity and equipped-item lists update after an item assignment. Use a TM and confirm the first window lists only compatible Pokémon that do not already have its move, the second window lists replacement slots, an open slot avoids replacing a move, and cancellation or Escape preserves the TM.
- On a route choice, confirm the Party panel's moves cannot be edited; equip and unequip held items, evolve an eligible Pokémon, and use a TM from Bag on a compatible recipient and chosen slot. Use Fire Stone on a low-level Vulpix and confirm it becomes Ninetales without a level requirement, plays the evolution scene, preserves active moves, and consumes one copy; confirm other species are ineligible and canceling the picker preserves the stone. After a level-up, confirm the XP screen requires a replacement or skip choice before route progression. Items purchased or returned from replacement become available at the next route choice.
- Review the shop, special, and result panels on the repeated tile floor at desktop and narrow widths; content should scroll within the translucent frame without losing its main action.
- Review XP cards for deployed participants and reserves: deployed battlers gain XP even if they faint, while reserves show zero XP and no level or move progression. Check a level-up, learned move, maximum-level Pokémon, and that the coin summary, milestone list, and OK action remain reachable with a full 20-Pokémon roster and reduced motion enabled.

### Route node art details

The selector uses code-native isometric cube tokens in `RouteScreen.tsx`, shaped to the supplied route-map reference: broad, flat diamond tops, pale stone side faces, a thin dark lower shadow, dark outlines, warm inset bevels, and restrained pixel-grain detail. The tops use caramel gold for battles and stores, rose red for elite encounters, green for healing, and blue for recruitment; the start is navy. Smaller cream emblems sit centered on each tile. The sword, healing cross, cart, question mark, recruit group, and boss mark are replaceable inside `Glyph`. A future icon asset should fit roughly 75×75 SVG units around its center and keep high contrast against its token. Turquoise route links stay visible behind nodes; available nodes receive a cyan rim and amber pointer. Locked future nodes remain fully colored so the route is readable, while their click and keyboard activation stay disabled. The number and placement of choices still come from the saved route graph, so a generated four-choice column may be denser than the visual reference.
